About Coral Sense · Amed, Bali

The ocean is not broken. Our relationship with it is.

We’re a coral restoration community in East Bali. We restore reefs, run 7 field experiments and bring ordinary people into ocean science.

Our approach

We don’t control the ocean. We ask it questions.

  • We create the conditions. Then we watch what the reef chooses on its own.
  • We never take healthy corals. We rescue broken fragments that would die without help and give them a second chance.
  • We bring people in. Travelers, students and kids plant corals and collect real data with us. People protect what they love, and love what they understand.
Why Bali

We’re in the heart of the Coral Triangle.

Only 6 countries share it: Indonesia, Malaysia, the Philippines, Papua New Guinea, the Solomon Islands and Timor-Leste. It’s home to more than 75% of known coral species and over a third of the world’s reef fish.

Our underwater lab

7 experiments running right now.

1 big question behind all of them: what gives the reef the most help with the least intervention? We repeat each test at 6 and 14 metres. No final answers yet, and we say so.

  1. 1
    Depth: 6 m vs 14 mThe same corals at 2 depths. Only 8 metres apart, but for a coral it’s like moving from Miami to Canada. More light and more heat up top. Cooler, calmer and darker below. Where do they do better?
  2. 2
    Clean fragment or the whole coral?Usually only the perfect healthy piece gets planted. We also plant corals with their scars and damaged base. Does the full colony recover better than we think?
  3. 3
    1 species or a mixed reef?A single-species reef grows simple and fast. Nature almost never builds one. Is diversity slower but stronger for the ocean of the future?
  4. 4
    What surface do corals choose?Bare metal or sand-coated structures. A reef starts with attachment, and young corals often pick a surface we didn’t expect.
  5. 5
    How close to plant?Too far and corals can’t support each other. Too close and they fight for light and space. We’re looking for the right distance.
  6. 6
    How often to clean?Some structures we clean often, some rarely, some never. Where does helping stop and interfering start?
  7. 7
    What settles on its own?Structures where we plant nothing at all. On just 2 of them, shaped like giant rainbows, 100+ young corals have already appeared by themselves.

Honest about restoration

Planting a few corals won’t save the ocean.

Here’s what restoration can do:

  • Save living fragments that would otherwise die on the sand.
  • Create new surfaces where corals can grow.
  • Buy time for global conditions and for science.
  • Produce real data on what works and what doesn’t.
Our own tool

CoralSense Lab.

An app we built for field science. After a dive, a diver speaks their notes and the app turns them into structured data: species, depth, damage, bleaching, experiment.

Voice → dataNotes after every dive, sorted automatically
Ocean conditionsEach record linked to temperature, waves and moon phase
Early signalsFlags unusual changes, like a sudden temperature jump

It checks observations against published reef science from open sources such as NOAA, AIMS and James Cook University. These are public materials, not formal partnerships.
